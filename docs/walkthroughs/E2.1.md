## §1 — The ABC check (the Section-1 verdict)

**Signature as found (Phase 0):** `fit(features, target) -> None`;
`predict(features) -> tuple[Any, Any]` returning `(mean, std)`.

**Verdict: no STOP — the ABC could EXPRESS paired uncertainty** (one call
returns both, structurally together) **but could not ENFORCE it**:
`(mean, None)`, a bare `(2, n)` array (which unpacks positionally into an
impersonating pair), and a 3-tuple all sailed through, so the pairing rule
was documentary. The revision keeps the signature and adds enforcement:

```
BEFORE (Phase 0)                      AFTER (E2.1)
@abstractmethod                       predict(features)   TEMPLATE METHOD,
predict(features) -> (mean, std)          final — __init_subclass__ refuses
                                          any subclass overriding it;
                                          validates: tuple, arity 2, no None
                                          member, shapes match, ndarray std
                                          finite and non-negative
                                      _predict(features)  <- the hook
                                          (abstract; consumers must never
                                           call it directly)
```

The `build()/_compute()` split `CovariateRecipe` already uses.
**References to the old shape, checked** (the E2.0 lesson — stale
references sit in tracked files): `engine.py:118` calls `predict()` and
needed no change; `test_engine_template_method.py`'s `StubEstimator`
renamed its override to `_predict` (updated in this commit, one line);
PATTERNS.md §4.1's "(mean, std) should stay the ABC's signature" now
carries a Realized-in-E2.1 note correcting its "unrepresentable" claim.

**The must-fix the adversarial review added to this verdict:** "final by
convention" was itself the express-but-not-enforce gap one level up — a
subclass overriding `predict()` bypassed all validation and still passed
the registry's isinstance gate (probe: a rogue `predict` returned a bare
`(2, 5)` array straight through the engine seam, unpacking into two
plausible rows). `__init_subclass__` now refuses the override at
class-definition time; a bypassing estimator cannot even be declared.

## §2 — Registry + baseline

**MeanBaselineEstimator:** predicts the training mean everywhere;
uncertainty is the training **SD (ddof=1), not SE** — predictive spread,
comparable to a kriging variance approaching the sill, where SE = SD/√n
answers "how well is the mean estimated" and shrinks toward false
confidence about individual locations. Two honesty caveats recorded in the
docstring (from the review, which otherwise confirmed the statistics): OK's
far-field variance exceeds the sill by the Lagrange term (iid analogue
SD·√(1+1/n), +1.4% at n=35 — plain SD kept for hand-computability), and
under our two-cluster geometry the sample SD is biased LOW relative to the
sill. Refuses: n<2 (SD undefined), non-finite y (isfinite, not isnan — an
inf target used to produce mean=inf, sd=NaN silently, the exact named
failure mode), 2-D y, predict-before-fit, and length-less features.
Constant y → SD=0 legitimately; the division hazard is E2.4's first
divider's to name (BACKLOG §3).

**EstimatorRegistry** (NormalizerRegistry's role, CovariateRegistry's
register conventions): `assert_complete()` makes "the baseline is
REGISTERED" structural — honest scope, per the review: registration is not
execution, and the execution half is E2.4's runner iterating `names()`
(never cherry-picking `get()`), recorded as a BACKLOG §3 obligation along
with the shared-stateful-instance and sd=0 cautions. Why a registry HERE is
a stronger case than the normalizer one: its completeness guards a
scientific-integrity rule (no claim without its floor), not a data-shape
accident.
