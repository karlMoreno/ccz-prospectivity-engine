## §1-STOP — the three decisions (Karl's; proposals below, then WAIT)

**1. Minimum pairs per lag bin.** Proposal: **≥ 30** (the standard
geostatistical rule of thumb for a usable lag estimate). On the real
data with the default edges this EXCLUDES 2–4 km (4 pairs — its 15.88 is
noise from four pairs spanning a bin with an internal zero-pair gap) and
8–10 km (19 pairs), leaving 0–2 (102), 4–6 (48), 6–8 (50), 10–13 (78),
and 986–997 (294). The softer alternative ≥ 15 would keep 8–10 and
exclude only 2–4.

**2. Which lags the fit may see.** Proposal: **within-cluster only
(0–13 km)**, with the 986–997 bin REPORTED beside the fit as the
far-field contrast, never fed to it. Consequences of each choice on this
data, qualitatively: *within-only* — the fitted range means "local
structure scale inside a cluster" (bounded by ~13 km); the sill is only
weakly constrained (the 10–13 bin exceeds the total variance), and the
model honestly claims nothing beyond 13 km — far predictions revert to
the mean with variance ≈ sill + Lagrange term. *Including 986–997* — the
sill anchors near the total variance (~16), but the range is then chosen
by the curve family's shape across a void with ZERO pairs: any value
from ~13 to ~986 km fits the same data nearly equally, so the parameter
would be an artifact of the family, not an estimate — two orders of
magnitude of pure assumption wearing a number.

**3. Model family + nugget.** Proposal: fit **exponential** (REQUIRED in
the family list regardless — `known_answer.py` plants an exponential
model, so §2's recovery test has nothing to recover otherwise), report
**spherical** alongside as the alternative fit; decline Gaussian (its
parabolic origin is unstable under exactly our conditions — large nugget,
few short-lag bins); defer Matérn (a third shape parameter fitted from
4–5 usable bins is overfitting by construction). **Nugget: FITTED**,
bounded [0, sill] — the data's headline IS the large nugget (~68% of
variance below 1 km); fixing it at zero would misstate the main finding.
If TS-6 parity requires matching TS-6's exact family, that is a
`[GEOLOGY — ISAAC]` question worth one look before Checkpoint 3.

*§2 (implementation, known-answer recovery, adversarial review) proceeds
only after all three answers.*

## §2 — Implementation (third commit)

**What was built:**

```
  coords (n,2) + y ─► bin_pairs (ONE binning: mean lag per bin, beyond-edge
       │                count, [lo,hi) edges validated) ─► EmpiricalVariogram
       │                                                        │
       │              fit_variogram: WLS on MEAN LAGS, weights = PAIR COUNTS,
       │              closed-form (nugget, ps) per candidate range on a 200-pt
       │              log grid, clamped non-negativity (proven optimal),
       │              first-wins tie-break; recorded exclusions per decision;
       │              flags: ceiling / floor / residual_dof / pairs_beyond
       ▼                                                        │
  OrdinaryKrigingEstimator._predict  ◄── FittedVariogram ◄──────┘
       [Γ 1; 1ᵀ 0][w; μ] = [γ₀; 1]   γ(0)=0 (exact interpolator)
       mean = wᵀy   var = wᵀγ₀ + μ (tolerance-bounded clamp)
       .report() → KrigingReport (everything E2.4 carries to provenance)
```

`OrdinaryKrigingEstimator` implements the E2.1 `_predict` hook — the ABC's
final `predict()` validates the pair, as designed. `fit()` is ATOMIC:
every piece of state is computed into locals and assigned in one block
after every refusal has had its chance (a review must-fix; see below).
Registered as `ordinary_kriging`; the baseline remains the only REQUIRED
entry.

**Directional / anisotropic variograms — DECLINED, explicitly.** With 35
points in two ~12 km clusters, directional binning would produce bins of
2–3 pairs (the omnidirectional 2–4 km bin already holds only 4). A
documented refusal, not an oversight; revisit only when between-cluster
sources land (BACKLOG §1 geographic-spread item).

**Honest far-field behaviour, not a defect to tune away.** With a fitted
range far shorter than the inter-cluster distance, kriging correctly
reverts toward the local mean far from data, with variance approaching —
and then EXCEEDING — the sill by the Lagrange term (only simple kriging
converges to the sill exactly). Both E2.1 caveats are now load-bearing and
in the estimator's docstring: OK's far-field variance sits ABOVE the sill
(pinned on fixture AND real data), and the baseline's sample SD is biased
LOW relative to the sill under our two-cluster geometry — so kriging's
far-field uncertainty and the baseline's SD are NOT the same number when
E2.4 puts them in one table.
