## §1 — The uncertainty method (REPORT; ends in a STOP)

Every estimator returns prediction AND uncertainty, paired — the E2.1
Template Method enforces it structurally. The question is HOW, and the two
candidates are not equivalent. Both were RUN, not argued.

### A fact that reframes the "zero new dependencies" option

**scikit-learn is not installed in this project.** It is on CLAUDE.md's
approved stack, and `pyproject.toml` says the approved ML libraries "land
with the phases that first use them" — E2.3 is that phase. So BOTH options
below add a dependency today; the difference is one package versus two.
The measurements were run in a throwaway venv, isolated from the project's
lockfile, so nothing was added before the decision.

### Option A — Quantile regression forest (`quantile-forest`, Zillow)

| Under the origin taxonomy | a TOOL, not data — the taxonomy classifies values, not code dependencies (recorded so it is not re-asked). |

**What it measures:** the conditional distribution of y given X, from the
per-leaf y values across all trees — per-point predictive intervals, not a
spread of tree means.

### Option B — Ensemble spread (SD of per-tree predictions), sklearn only

**What it measures:** disagreement among trees trained on bootstrap
resamples. What a consumer will read it as: predictive uncertainty. Those
differ, and on our data they differ badly (measured below).

### The measurement — known-answer covariate field, planted y = 2·X₃ + N(0, 1)

Holdout of 2,000 points of the SAME generated field; 500 trees; nominal 90%
intervals (spread as ±1.645·sd; QRF as its 5–95% quantiles).

| n | gen seed | true holdout RMSE | spread: mean sd → coverage | QRF: mean width (ideal 3.29) → coverage | spread + residual-SD floor → coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 500 | 42 | 1.049 | 0.959 → **0.85** | 3.11 → **0.84** | 1.038 → **0.89** |
| 500 | 7 | 1.078 | 0.970 → 0.84 | 3.11 → 0.84 | 1.047 → 0.88 |
| 35 | 42 | 1.280 | 1.153 → 0.86 | 3.54 → 0.81 | 1.247 → 0.89 |
| 35 | 7 | 1.272 | 0.841 → **0.73** | 2.56 → **0.69** | 0.917 → 0.77 |
| 35 | 11 | 1.222 | 1.256 → 0.90 | 3.89 → 0.85 | 1.334 → 0.92 |

Read: on WELL-SEPARATED X, spread and QRF are both mildly under-covered
and roughly comparable; at n=35 both swing seed to seed (0.73–0.90 and
0.69–0.85). Neither wins here. The floor option is the most calibrated
row — but see its caveat.

### THE MEASUREMENT THAT MATTERS — rank-4 X, mimicking today's real matrix

35 rows, only FOUR distinct X rows (14/7/7/7, the E2.0-2 occupancy), y
varying WITHIN each group with the real cell-group means and sd ≈ 3.2:

| Quantity, at the 4 distinct rows | Values |
|---|---|
| True within-group sd (irreducible to ANY X-model) | 2.61 · 3.68 · 2.81 · 3.52 |
| **Ensemble spread** (SD of tree means) | **0.68 · 1.46 · 1.03 · 1.37** — understates by 2–4× |
| QRF 5–95% half-width, as an sd-equivalent | 2.70 · 3.08 · 1.93 · 2.83 — tracks the truth |
| Residual sd (in-bag training fit) | 2.93 — near the pooled within-group sd 3.06 |

**This is the prompt's warning made numeric.** With four distinct rows,
trees agree for bad reasons — they are all fitting the same 4 examples —
so the spread of tree MEANS collapses to a fraction of the real predictive
spread. A consumer reading it as predictive uncertainty is misled by a
factor of 2–4. QRF, which returns the conditional distribution of the y
VALUES in the leaves rather than the tree means, recovers the within-group
spread by construction.

### Option C — spread + a stated floor: honest, and here is where it fails

Combining spread with an in-bag residual SD floor was the best-calibrated
row on separated X. But its floor is the TRAINING residual — 0.39 at n=500
against a true noise sd of 1.0 (a forest overfits in-bag by design), so on
separated X the floor is 40% of the truth and the calibration was partly
luck. The honest floor is the OOB residual (1.06 — accurate), and OOB is
RANDOM resampling: on autocorrelated data it is spatial leakage, the
failure mode this project's methodology exists to refuse. Using OOB inside
the uncertainty would smuggle a leaked quantity into a validation-facing
number by another door — exactly what §2's structural OOB rule exists to
prevent. On rank-4 X the in-bag residual (2.93) happens to be near the
truth, but that is a property of this degenerate fixture, not of the
method. **Not defensible as a general rule; recorded so it is not offered
decoratively.**

### Recommendation

**Option A — quantile regression forest.** Its uncertainty is the
conditional distribution, which is what E2.4's comparison table means by
"uncertainty" for the other two estimators (the baseline's SD is a
predictive spread; kriging's variance is a predictive variance) — the
three columns would then answer the same question. On today's rank-4 data
the ensemble spread is wrong by 2–4× in the flattering direction, and no
in-house floor fixes that without either overfitting (in-bag) or leaking
(OOB). The dependency cost is one Apache-2.0 line on top of the
scikit-learn line E2.3 adds regardless, with no transitive additions, no
version conflict, and a binary wheel.

If Option B is chosen anyway, the estimator MUST report the spread under a
name that states what it is (e.g. `tree_disagreement_sd_not_predictive`)
and the walkthrough must carry the 2–4× understatement as a standing caveat
on every RF uncertainty column E2.4 prints.

**STOP.** The choice — dependency policy and uncertainty semantics at once
— is Karl's. §2 does not begin until answered.

## §2 — Implementation (second commit; adversarially reviewed)

### The two facts, reported before the code

**Fact 1 — the ceiling binds.** n = 35, k = 8, and on today's fixture X
has **FOUR distinct rows** (centered matrix rank **3** — four points span
a 3-D affine subspace). The E2.0-3 ceiling R² = 0.348 binds this
estimator: co-celled stations have identical X and different y,
irreducible to any covariate model. Importance is arithmetic over 4
examples. Kriging is exempt (coordinates); the asymmetry is on record in
the E2.0 closeout and will matter when E2.4 tables both.

**Fact 2 — the covariates are strongly collinear by construction.** All 8
derive from one DEM. The correlation matrix on the real TrainingMatrix:

```
                   depth slope aspect rough  prof   plan   tpi   bpi
  depth             1.00  0.04 -0.76  0.17  0.94  0.97  0.96  0.98
  slope             0.04  1.00 -0.02 -0.73  0.23  0.27 -0.00 -0.03
  aspect           -0.76 -0.02  1.00 -0.57 -0.90 -0.80 -0.90 -0.86
  roughness         0.17 -0.73 -0.57  1.00  0.22  0.07  0.37  0.34
  profile_curvature 0.94  0.23 -0.90  0.22  1.00  0.98  0.97  0.96
  plan_curvature    0.97  0.27 -0.80  0.07  0.98  1.00  0.95  0.95
  tpi               0.96 -0.00 -0.90  0.37  0.97  0.95  1.00  1.00
  bpi               0.98 -0.03 -0.86  0.34  0.96  0.95  1.00  1.00
```

**12 of 28 pairs have |r| > 0.9**; tpi/bpi correlate at 1.00 (on 4
distinct rows they are the same column up to affine map). Consequence:
collinear features split credit unstably, so a single-seed importance
ranking is noise wearing a bar chart — `report()` carries importance PER
SEED, and the churn is measured below.

### What was built

```
  X (n,k), y ──► RandomForestEstimator.fit  (ATOMIC, the E2.2 lesson)
                   ├─ RandomForestQuantileRegressor(seed, n_estimators=500,
                   │     max_samples_leaf=None ← the ONE non-default)
                   ├─ importance under `importance_seeds` (extra forests)
                   └─ OOB only if compute_oob_diagnostic=True
                 ._predict → (mean, sd) of the CONDITIONAL DISTRIBUTION
                   (199-point quantile grid, aggregate_leaves_first=False)
                 .predict_quantiles → interval face of the same distribution
                 .report → RandomForestReport
                   .validation_facing_fields()  ← explicit ALLOW-LIST
                   .oob_diagnostic_not_validation  ← OUTSIDE it, by name
```

Registered as `random_forest`; the baseline remains the only REQUIRED
entry.

**Uncertainty semantics delivered (the §1 claim, checked on the rank-4
fixture — numbers RE-DERIVED after the review's must-fix, see below):**
QRF sd at the four distinct rows **2.52 / 3.38 / 2.57 / 3.25** against
true within-group sd0 2.51 / 3.41 / 2.60 / 3.26 — ratios 1.00 / 0.99 /
0.99 / 1.00 — where the ensemble spread of per-tree means is 0.69 / 1.50 /
1.01 / 1.38 (sd/spread 2.3–3.7×, the §1 finding now measured on the test
fixture itself). Means match the group means to < 0.1. On DISTINCT X
(the §1 known-answer field, n=35, planted noise sd 1.0): mean sd 1.11,
90% coverage 0.84. Deterministic under a seed.

**The pre-review code was wrong, and the rank-4 fixture could not see
it (the review's must-fix — CLAUDE.md rule 4 exactly).** The WIP commit
passed quantile-forest `aggregate_leaves_first=False`, which computes
each TREE's quantile function and AVERAGES them — not the pooled
Meinshausen distribution the docstring described. On any training set
with DISTINCT X rows every leaf holds one y, every per-tree quantile
function is flat, and **the sd is identically 0** (reviewer probe on the
§1 field: sd max 0.0000, 90% coverage 0.000; the same forest under
`True`: sd 1.12, coverage 0.85 — which is how §1's own table was
produced, since that probe used the library default). On rank-4 X it
understated the pooled sd by 17–31% (2.08 / 2.39 / 1.80 / 2.24), which
the pre-review docstring misattributed to "bootstrap shrinkage". Today's
corpus is co-celled, so the sd was nonzero by accident of the fixture;
the day a corpus with distinct covariate rows arrives, RF uncertainty
would have been 0 and the pairing template (finite, non-negative) would
have blessed it. Fixed: `aggregate_leaves_first=True, weighted_leaves=
True` (Meinshausen eq. 5–7), the mean from quantile-forest's exact
`quantiles="mean"`, and a DISTINCT-X test that pins sd > 0 near the
planted noise — the observer the rank-4 fixture structurally lacked.

**The RF2 record, corrected twice.** Mutation RF2 (`max_samples_leaf` →
1) left the pre-review suite green; the author's first explanation ("leaf-
1 is a random-subsample approximation, less bootstrap-shrunk") was
half-right: quantile-forest's leaf-1 IS a random one-per-leaf-per-tree
subsample, but its predict path POOLS across trees regardless of the
aggregation flag — so the comparison was pooled-leaf-1 vs quantile-
averaged-retain-all, apples to oranges. Under the correct pooled setting
retain-all is 0.99–1.00× the truth and leaf-1 0.94–1.02×: retain-all is
both the definition AND empirically closer. And RF2 was invisible for a
second reason the review found: `report()` ECHOED the constructor literal
`max_samples_leaf: None` instead of reading it back from the fitted
forest — a mutated forest carried a FALSE provenance record with no test
failing. `report()` now reads `get_params()` and `len(estimators_)`; RF2
and RF5 (n_estimators forced) both fail by name through the report test.

**The OOB hard rule, structural.** OOB is computed only on explicit
request, carried ONLY under `oob_diagnostic_not_validation` (docstring:
random resampling = spatial leakage on autocorrelated data), and
`validation_facing_fields()` is an explicit allow-list — a future field is
not validation-facing until added on purpose, so OOB cannot arrive by
omission. The test (strengthened by the review) asserts with OOB COMPUTED
(a real float exists to leak): no OOB-shaped name anywhere; a RECURSIVE
leaf walk finds no value equal to the OOB float or its cheap derivations
(1−oob, 100·oob, rounded, negated); and the STRUCTURAL check — an OOB-on
and an OOB-off estimator under the same seed produce IDENTICAL validation-
facing structures, so nothing in them can have been computed from OOB.
The production registry instance is pinned OOB-off. Honest limit, stated
(review): the rule holds for `report()`/`predict()`/`predict_quantiles()`;
the underscore-private `_forest` can still be asked for OOB by a caller
who reaches around the estimator — E2.5's refuse-to-validate guard is the
claim-time backstop, and E2.4's runner must consume `report()`, never
`_forest`.

### Importance instability — measured on the real matrix, 10 seeds, 500 trees

```
  seed 0: aspect .331  slope .320  profile_curvature .242
  seed 1: aspect .361  slope .290  profile_curvature .247
  ...
  seed 7: slope  .315  aspect .313  profile_curvature .252
  seed 9: profile_curvature .304  aspect .304  slope .281
  top-1 across seeds: aspect 8, slope 1, profile_curvature 1
  rank ranges: aspect 1–2 · slope 1–3 · profile_curvature 1–3 ·
               roughness 4–4 · depth/plan/tpi/bpi 5–8
```

**The ranking is rock-stable in its top-3 SET, and that is itself the
suspicious result the prompt anticipated — with the MECHANISM corrected by
the review.** The author's first reading ("choosing among ~3 effective
directions, so importance cannot churn") was audited and refuted: random
rank-4 fixtures with a KNOWN generating column churn UNIFORMLY across
seeds (all 8 features 0.15–0.22, top-1 changing seed to seed) and never
reliably place the generating column — or its exact affine twin — first.
So 4-row degeneracy per se produces INSTABILITY, and the stability on the
real matrix has a different, more mundane cause: with four X rows, split
candidates are the ORDERING ENDPOINTS of each column, and sklearn's
absolute 1e-7 split threshold on the ~1e-6-magnitude curvature values
freezes which columns can even be split — a unit change on
plan_curvature moves it into the top group with IDENTICAL predictions.
Either way the conclusion holds: the top-3 stability is an artifact of
the 4-row support, not evidence of a robust signal, and importance claims
on the real 35-station matrix are unsupported. Within the top 3 the order
does churn (three different top-1 features across ten seeds).

**Two numbers that close the loop:** RF in-sample R² on the real matrix =
**0.348 — the E2.0-3 ceiling exactly** (a full-depth forest on 4 distinct
rows memorizes the four group means; independent confirmation the ceiling
arithmetic is right). The OOB diagnostic, requested for the record only:
R² = 0.161 — a RANDOM-resample number, NOT validation, printed here once
so its magnitude is on the page and never in a claim.
