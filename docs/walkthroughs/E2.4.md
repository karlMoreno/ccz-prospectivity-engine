### 1A — Fold assignment for leave-one-cluster-out: the rule, and why it cannot be tie-break-sensitive

**Rule.** Blocks are the connected components of the graph joining station
pairs within `linkage_km` (great-circle, `haversine_km`) — single linkage.
One fold per block: hold the block out, train on every other row. Default
linkage = `geometry.DEFAULT_LINKAGE_KM` = 100 km, the corpus manifest's own
clustering, so `spatial_summary_training_eligible.clusters == 2` and "LOCO
has two folds" are ONE computation (`single_linkage_labels`), not two that
happen to agree.

**Why no tie-break exists.** Connected components are a property of the
graph, not of the order edges are visited. The partition changes ONLY when
the threshold crosses a minimum-spanning-tree edge length; between two
consecutive MST edges it is identical. On the real 35 stations the MST's
last two edges are **10.018 km** and **986.036 km**, so any linkage in
**[10.018, 986.036)** — a 976 km plateau with 100 km near its logarithmic
middle — yields the SAME two folds. `FoldAssignment.stability_interval_km`
records that interval per split, so the claim is a manifest fact, not a
sentence here. Tested at both edges EXACTLY (bit-identical: the same
haversine computes the edge and the threshold): linkage == 10.018 km
reproduces the assignment; linkage == 986.036 km merges everything and the
splitter refuses by name ("1 block").

```
  fold holdout_block_1(n=14, W)   train E 21 ──► predict W 14   min sep 986.04 km
  fold holdout_block_0(n=21, E)   train W 14 ──► predict E 21   min sep 986.04 km
```

**"sd = 0" and "e = 0" are NUMERICAL, not bitwise (§1 review, should-fix).**
The two LIVE sources do not produce bitwise zeros: a constant-y baseline's
`std(ddof=1)` is ~1e-15 whenever the mean is not exactly representable
(seven copies of the real abundance 19.6 → 3.8e-15, not 0.0), and
quantile-forest's weighted mean of a single-valued leaf can sit 1 ulp off
its constant. A bitwise `== 0.0` would MISS the first source (a
confidently-wrong prediction under sd 1e-15 would come out as a finite,
astronomically large z with status ok) and flag the second as confidently
WRONG (a 1-ulp residual). Both are defined through `ZERO_TOL_REL = 1e-9`
relative to the point's scale max(\|y\|, \|p\|) — one part in 10⁹, twelve
orders of magnitude below any measured abundance difference: a floating-
point equality, NOT a statistical tolerance, stated in every result's
`details`, and pinned by a test on exactly those two sources.

Every value is a `MetricValue(value, status, n, details)`: `value is None`
⇔ `status != "ok"` (enforced), and a non-finite value is REFUSED at
construction — verified on pydantic 2.13.4: `model_dump_json()` renders

substance hash unchanged. Not
in the set, deliberately: CRPS / log score (assume a distributional form the
three sd semantics do not share — a quantile half-width is not a Gaussian
σ) and interval scores at other levels (need quantiles the baseline and
kriging do not report). Coverage at ±1σ is the one calibration number all
three semantics support; the uncertainty-semantics column (obligation 7)
sits beside every sd-shaped number in §3's tables.

### The known-answer leakage number (tests/test_cv_known_answer.py)

Two 8×8 grids at 1.5 km spacing, 60 km apart, a GP with exponential
covariance, effective range 8 km ≪ the 50 km gap and ≫ the spacing, sill 10,
nugget 0.5 (seed 13). Leave-one-cluster-out (30 km linkage) vs random 8-fold,
three estimators, the same field: **rmse(random) / rmse(LOCO) = baseline
0.814 · kriging 0.568 · RF 0.468.** The baseline cannot leak spatial
structure — only the cluster-mean shift — so its ratio is the honest floor of
"how much easier random folds are"; the spatial models' ratios below it
(by 0.25 / 0.35) are the inflation random splitting buys. Pinned with
margins (≤ 0.65 / ≤ 0.60 / floor ≥ 0.75 / gap ≥ 0.15) and at the measured
values ± 0.02. The companion test pins the theorem on the fixture: on both
LOCO folds kriging's RMSE and bias equal the baseline's (range ≪ gap).
