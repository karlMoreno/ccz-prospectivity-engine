## §1 — The guard

```
  RunManifest + the design being claimed + the feature-stack manifest
                          │
                          ▼
        Precondition (Enum, 6 members) ── _CHECKERS (6)
        exhaustive BOTH WAYS, asserted AT IMPORT:
          every member has a guard   (a rule nobody enforces)
          every guard has a member   (a refusal nobody can discover)
                          │  each checker raises ClaimRefused(precondition, msg)
                          ▼
     evaluate_claim() ─────────────────► ClaimVerdict
       every result, PASS and FAIL        .eligible   ← DERIVED: not self.failures
       + the watermark                    .is_scientific ← eligible AND unwatermarked
                          │
                          ▼
     require_validated_claim() raises naming EVERY failing precondition
```

Six preconditions, each with its own refusal message:

| # | Precondition | Reads |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | spatially-blocked CV ran | the design record's `spatially_blocked` **declaration** — never the design's name; and cross-checks the manifest's own `claim_eligible_designs` |
| 2 | the mean baseline ran in every fold | a baseline row per fold, each `scored` |
| 3 | every prediction carries a paired uncertainty | equal lengths, finite, non-negative |
| 4 | all feature layers share one DEM | `_DEM_IDENTITY_FIELDS` imported from the extraction guard — one list, two call sites, so build-time and claim-time cannot drift |
| 5 | provenance records corpus + feature-stack + training-matrix hashes | `upstream_hashes`, and that each is a `sha256:` identity, not a name |
| 6 | an acceptance threshold existed BEFORE the scores | Contract 8 via `acceptance_thresholds()`: absent / null / AUTHORED / `set_after_scores` / post-dating `scores_first_visible` are five distinct refusals |

**The timestamp's honesty limit is stated where the comparison happens**
(`_check_pre_registered_threshold`, not only in a doc): `scores_first_visible`
sits outside the substance hash, so it is MUTABLE METADATA, nothing detects a
hand-edited value, and the comparison is honest **by convention, not by
mechanism** — the COMMIT that introduced the scores is the authoritative
witness. The guard performs the comparison anyway, because a convention that
is checked is worth more than one only written down, and it says so at the
site.

**Refusal vs watermark — the load-bearing distinction.** The watermark is NOT
a precondition. A synthetic-origin run that satisfies all six is **eligible AND
watermarked**: `eligible` stays true, `is_scientific` is false, and a note
travels with the verdict. The watermark DERIVES from the computed origin
(`run_watermark` → `matrix_watermark`, P2.0d-3) — no flag was added and no
derivation re-implemented. `eligible` is a DERIVED property, not a stored
field, so "not eligible with nothing failing" cannot be represented; the first
thing that would have drifted into a stored flag is exactly this watermark.

The verbatim refusal on the headline design:

```
[an_acceptance_threshold_existed_before_the_scores] REFUSING to emit run
'e2.4-report' (design 'leave_one_site_out') as a validated claim: 1 of 6
preconditions failed.
  - [an_acceptance_threshold_existed_before_the_scores] no pre-registered gate
    existed when these scores were computed. model_config.yaml has no
    acceptance_thresholds field — Contract 8's header records that it 'arrives
    with E2.5's refuse-to-validate guard', and the guard is here, but the SLOT

    it in the contracts README) and the VALUE is Track G's. Until both happen,
    no acceptance gate exists and no run can be emitted as a validated claim.
```

**Three independent reasons, each correct**, exactly as the task predicted:
no pre-registered gate existed (precondition 6, on every design); the
covariates are synthetic so every output is watermarked non-scientific; and
schemes A and random k-fold are refused BY RECORD at precondition 1.

**What PASSES is the part that proves the guard discriminates.** On both
claim-eligible designs, preconditions 1–5 hold on real recorded evidence —
`leave_one_site_out` at a measured 4.614 km minimum separation over 5 folds,
the baseline scored in all 5, 97 predictions each with a finite non-negative
paired sd, 8 layers on one DEM, three sha256 upstream hashes. A guard that
returned "refused" unconditionally would look identical on the failing rows and
different on these.

Nothing was softened, no override exists, and no precondition was tuned toward
passing. **The machinery is correct and the data is not yet publishable —
which is precisely what Phase 2 set out to be able to say.**

## The phase's findings, in one place

**1. The covariate channel is saturated at four effective examples — the
0.348 ceiling, through three instruments.** 35 stations occupy 4 distinct
covariate cells on today's fixture DEM, so the maximum covariate-model R² is
0.348, and it showed up three separate ways: in the PREDICTIONS (RF's in-sample
R² sits exactly at the ceiling), in the IMPORTANCES (8 covariates arguing over
4 examples; rank order churns across seeds), and in the UNCERTAINTIES (QRF
leaves that can be single-valued — 0 of 35 today, pinned so the day it changes
is visible). Under honest validation it is worse: RF's held-out R² is
**−0.172** at the within-cluster gate and **−0.656** across clusters. Expires
at Checkpoint 1, when ~460 m GEBCO cells re-separate X into 35 distinct rows.

**2. ~68% of the abundance variance is unstructured below station spacing —
`[GEOLOGY — ISAAC]`, the first Phase-2 output with a geology reading.** E2.2
measured γ(0–1 km) = 10.2 against a total variance of 15.1 on REAL coordinates
and REAL abundance. Two box cores a few hundred metres apart differ, on
average, by most of the field's total spread. This is a measured statement
about nodule patchiness at sub-2-km scales.

**FOR ISAAC, stated plainly (sharpened at the E2.5 approval).** This is the
one Phase-2 number that MEASURES THE NODULE FIELD and owes nothing to the
synthetic DEM: real station coordinates, real abundance, **no covariates in
the calculation at all** — a variogram of y against distance. Everything a
covariate touches is a noise-score until Track G delivers real terrain, and
the whole run is watermarked non-scientific for exactly that reason; this
number is outside that shadow. **It is therefore the only Phase-2 measurement
Isaac can act on today.**

Said precisely, because "the only DEM-independent number" would be too
strong: the five-site geometry (finding 3) is DEM-independent too, and so is
every kriging and baseline SCORE — kriging declares
`input_kind = "coordinates"` (`estimators/kriging.py:133`) and the baseline
ignores feature values by construction (`estimators/mean_baseline.py:50–61`),
so only RF's numbers ride on the synthetic covariates. But the geometry
describes where the ship stopped, and the scores describe how a method
performed; **the nugget is the only one that describes the nodules.** That is
what makes it actionable rather than merely real: at ~68% unstructured below
station spacing, sub-kilometre replication is the sampling change that would
move the answer, and no amount of better terrain data will.

**3. The corpus is five SITES, not two clusters** (E2.4 §1): MST edge lengths
are thirty at 0.054–0.857 km, then 4.614, 6.219, 10.018, 986.036. E = three
sites (6/8/7 stations), W = two (7/7). This is what made an honest
within-cluster gate possible at all, and it is a property of the corpus
coordinates rather than of the fixture.

**4. The leakage demonstration, with two witnesses.** REAL: letting a
≤ 0.86 km site-mate into training turns kriging's within-cluster uplift from
**−0.00 into +0.36** (R² −0.219 → +0.127) and RF's from **+0.05 into +0.46**.
SYNTHETIC, on a planted field with a known 8 km range: rmse(random 8-fold) /
rmse(leave-one-cluster-out) = **0.568** for kriging against a baseline floor of
**0.814** — the baseline cannot leak spatial structure, so the gap below its
floor is the inflation. Same phenomenon, one real and one planted. (Seed
caveat: the DIRECTION holds at 8 of 8 sampled seeds; the pinned magnitudes are
seed 13's — BACKLOG §3.)

**5. THE HEADLINE: neither model beats the mean baseline at the honest gate.**
At leave-one-site-out (4.6–10 km), kriging's pooled uplift is **−0.00**
(ratio 1.000) and RF's **+0.05** (0.989); both pooled R² are negative. Two of
kriging's four fits report the fitter's own *no structure* verdict, and a fifth
fold refuses outright. **This is a finding about the data — see finding 2 —
not a failure of the code.** Across clusters, kriging ≈ baseline is a theorem
(the fitted range ≤ 13 km against a 991 km gap), not an outcome; the
across-cluster fold measures that the two clusters differ by 1.886 kg/m² and
cannot rank estimators.

## What Checkpoint 2 CAN and CANNOT review

**CAN:** the machinery. Every seam is exercised end to end on real
coordinates and real abundance — the corpus build, the training matrix and its
manifest, three estimators behind one ABC, four CV designs behind one splitter
Strategy, the runner's eight obligations, a committed run artifact whose
science reproduces byte-for-byte, and a claim guard that refuses for stated
reasons. Also reviewable: the two real-data measurements that do not depend on
the synthetic DEM — the 68% nugget (finding 2) and the five-site geometry
(finding 3), both from coordinates and y alone.

**CANNOT:** any model claim. Three independent blocks, and all three are
correct: (a) **no acceptance gate exists**, so no threshold was ever
pre-registered and every threshold set from now on is post-hoc for this dataset
— permanently, and for kriging in the full sense, since it fits real
coordinates against real y; (b) **the covariates are synthetic**, so every
RF number is a noise-score and every output is watermarked non-scientific;
(c) the only claim-eligible designs are the two spatially-blocked ones, and at
the honest one neither model beats the floor. Checkpoint 2 can review whether
the engine is trustworthy. It cannot review a result, because there is not yet
a publishable one — and the guard now says so structurally rather than in prose.
