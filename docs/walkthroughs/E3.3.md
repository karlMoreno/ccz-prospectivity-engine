## §2 — Commit 2: the agreement

Per Karl's decision (E3.0 §5): **descriptive r with N_eff printed beside it,
NO p-value**, and THE LIMIT travels in the output itself (`INFLATION_NOTE`,
asserted verbatim by a test): *a correction adjusts degrees of freedom; it
cannot manufacture information — the real problem is a surface near-constant
over ~99% of its domain, and no test corrects for that.*

**N_eff** is Clifford–Richardson-style: `N² / Σ ρ̂A·ρ̂B` over binned empirical
correlograms of the two compared surfaces, clamped to [1, N]. The two-sided
known-answer pins both behaviours: a smooth pair collapses below N/10, a
white-noise pair stays above N/2 — separating "collapses under shared
smoothness" from "always small" and from "always N".

### Two findings from running the real comparison

1. **The baseline's constant surface has sd = 3.55e-15, not 0.0.** Mean
   subtraction leaves ULP noise on every element, so an exact-zero
   degeneracy test let the constant surface through to `np.corrcoef`, which
   returned an r made entirely of that noise, labelled "ok". Fixed with the
   E2.4 `ZERO_TOL_REL` numerical-zero policy at both sites (the correlation
   and N_eff, so the two verdicts cannot disagree), and the degenerate-case
   test uses the baseline's REAL surface so the noise stays in the test's
   path.
2. **n_eff ≈ N against today's fixture is the formula being right, not the
   inflation failing to register.** The synthetic TS-6 is WHITE NOISE, and
   Clifford–Richardson honestly reports the naive df as nearly right when
   one field is white — the product `ρ̂A·ρ̂B` vanishes when either factor
   does. **The E3.0 "N_eff ≈ 2" expectation assumed BOTH surfaces smooth**,
   which is the real-TS-6 case, not the fixture's. Recorded here and in the
   test's docstring in advance, so a later reader cannot report the small-N
   collapse (or its absence today) as a finding.
